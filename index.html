About

Engineer & Builder

I'm Abhinav Abraham, an engineer who lives at the intersection of autonomous systems, machine learning, and physical hardware. I like problems that don't stay solved on a whiteboard - where a sensor package has to survive vibration and pressure, a perception model has to run in real time, and a navigation stack has to actually get a robot from A to B. Ruggedized enclosures, custom PCBs, and the ML pipelines that sit downstream of them are where I spend most of my time.

My Ph.D. research at the University of Illinois Chicago spans closed-loop control, real-time ML inference pipelines, embedded electronics, and multi-modal sensing, carried out as part of a DoD-funded program (VICTOR) alongside UW-Madison and the U.S. Army CCDC-ARL. The sensor I designed is now field-deployed inline with a running engine test cell, and the ML pipeline I built for it turns raw spectra into a usable property estimate in under six seconds.

During my M.S., I built a fully autonomous closed-loop system for zebrafish neuroscience: fluorescence imaging fed a classifier that predicted movement intent with 86.7% accuracy, and that prediction drove a robotic actuator in real time - no human in the loop. That sense-compute-actuate pattern has stuck with me as a design principle ever since.

Outside formal research, I build things for their own sake: agentic AI pipelines, ROS 2 navigation stacks, an EKF state estimator tested against real UAV flight data, and computer vision benchmarks - all up on GitHub if you want to look under the hood.

AutoCrystalLab - Closed-Loop Crystal Characterization
A computational framework for iterative crystal characterization and experiment planning using powder X-ray diffraction (PXRD) data. Extracts spectral descriptors, builds Gaussian-process surrogate models with uncertainty quantification, and runs multi-objective Bayesian optimization to rank candidate experiments. Benchmarks random, uncertainty-driven, and Bayesian sampling strategies across sequential experimental campaigns, with Bayesian sampling cutting required physical test iterations by up to 80% versus random sampling. Includes an interactive visualization dashboard for PXRD similarity analysis, optimization trajectories, and phase-space exploration.
